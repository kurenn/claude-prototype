What it actually does

Six things that keep it from looking generated.

why

layout · Editorial is 2-up, Index is a dense 3-up. That's the Layout control below.

01

Interactive discovery

It asks how the thing should feel before it builds. A short Q&A on tone, inspiration, audience and scope drives real, domain-matched content, and it confirms the design direction with you before writing a single screen.

02

Real design files, not guesses

It greps 84 vendored real-brand DESIGN.md files for your brand or domain, reads up to three, and takes their free fonts and design DNA, never the brand. Tone-keyed font and palette menus are the fallback, with guards against the category reflex.

03

A QA loop that critiques itself

A pre-emit self-critique scores every screen on six hostile-reviewer axes, 37 slop-gate lint rules run before handoff, and a blind design judge ranks rendered output. Assess is a completion gate you can't skip.

04

Zero dependencies, no build

Plain HTML, Tailwind via CDN and a few vanilla JS files, deployable to any static host as it is. View Transitions, a fake-latency loading engine and three motion tiers come built in, all degrading cleanly under reduced motion.

05

The always-on control bar

Every prototype ships one visible bar: Theme, Layout, Persona, Motion, Loading, Share and Feedback. It never wraps to a second line, and reviewers judge the options they can see. It's the one at the bottom of this page.

06

Decisions stated on purpose

Signature move, icon stance, imagery stance, motion tier: every prototype names them in its DESIGN.md. Design by decision, not by accident, the way the notes in this page's margin do.

The whole point

One of these looks generated. One was decided.
