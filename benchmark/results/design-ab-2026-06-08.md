Method: `render.sh` → blind pairwise judge per `design-judge.md`. A/B mapping randomized
per brief and hidden from each judge.

## Results

| Brief | Winner (decoded) | Lens tally | Decided mainly by |

| pulse | **baseline** | 6–2 (2 ties) | candidate tripped anti-slop purple-on-black; candidate mobile toggles off-screen |

**Win count: baseline 2, candidate 1.**

## Honest interpretation

- The judge **discriminated every time** (6–2, never a wash) — Tier 3 works; it sees what
  the mechanical scorer (all six builds 163–168/168) cannot.
- But the deciding factors were **per-run content-integrity defects** (placeholder leaks,
  count contradictions, alt-text leaks, anti-slop color, off-screen mobile controls), **not
  a consistent design-taste gap attributable to the refactor.** The aesthetic lenses
  (hierarchy, spacing, typography) were frequently close or tied. At n=1/brief these
  defects are a quality lottery, not a skill-version signal.
- **Both** skill versions produced one notably flawed build and ~two solid ones. The
  candidate produced the single cleanest build (ledgerline) and the single most-flawed
  content (tend placeholders). That spread is consistent with run variance dominating.

## Systemic defects affecting BOTH versions (real skill/template bugs — highest-value fix)

1. **Mobile horizontal overflow at 390px on all 6 builds** — pills, primary buttons, and
   (worst) table amount columns run off-screen. This is a scaffold/skill issue, independent
   of the refactor, and hurts every prototype. **Fix this before re-running the A/B.**
2. **Fixed control bar occludes content on desktop** on several builds (last table row;
   on candidate/pulse and baseline/ledgerline it covered live dashboard text).

## Verdict

No evidence the progressive-disclosure refactor **systematically** harms design quality.
The 2–1 baseline lean is within noise for n=1/brief and is driven by per-run defects, not a
design gap. The refactor's proven win (−73% trigger-time context) stands. The most useful
thing this run surfaced is the **mobile-overflow + control-bar occlusion** pair, which is
worth fixing in the skill regardless of the refactor.

To turn the 2–1 into a real claim either way, re-run at 2–3 builds per side per brief
**after** fixing mobile overflow (so the judge isn't deciding on a shared defect).
